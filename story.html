Maison Aube

Our story

It started with a coat, and a question: what would it take to make one properly, and to be honest about every step?

Concept copy. Maison Aube is a fictional label imagined by webtheory.co to show how a slow-fashion shop could read. The people, places, runs and methods on this page are invented; nothing is for sale.

i.

The name

Aube is French for dawn. The dye house starts at first light, when the baths are cool and the river is quiet, and the name came from there.

Most of our pictures, as it happens, are of the other end of the day. Dusk is when the valley turns the colours we dye in, so that is the hour we frame everything in: an arch, a low sun, the hills going from ochre to rust to almost black.

ii.

Small runs

We make each piece once a season, in a single run. The number is decided before anything is cut, from how much cloth the mill can weave well and how much our workshop can sew without hurrying.

When a run sells through, we don't reorder. We wait for next year, and usually change something: a longer sleeve, a heavier cloth, a pocket moved an inch. Every product page tells you how many were made, so you know what you are buying into.

iii.

Colour from plants

Madder, weld, walnut and indigo, grown or gathered within a day's drive of the dye house. No synthetic dyes and no optical brighteners.

Plant dyes behave like weather. The same recipe gives a slightly different red in a wet spring than a dry one, and indigo keeps changing for as long as you wear it. We would rather show you that than sell you a colour that never moves.

  • MadderRoots, dug in their third year.
  • WeldThe whole plant, cut in flower.
  • WalnutGreen hulls, gathered in autumn.
  • IndigoLeaves, fermented in a vat.

iv.

Made to be mended

Seams are cut with generous allowances so a tailor can let them out or take them in. Every piece comes with spare buttons and a card of matching thread, dyed in the same bath.

Clothes that are meant to last need looking after, so every product page ends with care notes written by the people who made the piece, not by a label printer.

v.

A shop that reads slowly

We built this shop to be read like a lookbook: the landscape first, then the pieces, then everything we know about each one.

There are no countdowns, no pop-ups and no discount banners. When you are ready to buy, the bag keeps out of your way: a size, a quantity, a total, and nothing else.
