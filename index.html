I — The idea

Fewer things, made slowly, and explained properly.

Most clothes shops look alike: a grid of products and a banner with a percentage on it. That works if you buy often and quickly. It helps much less if you would rather buy one good coat and keep it for ten years.

So this shop reads more like a lookbook. Each collection begins with the landscape it came from. Each piece tells you what it is made of, who made it, how long it took and how to look after it. When you are ready, the bag is one tap away, and checkout keeps out of your way.

  1. i.

    Small runs

    Each piece is made once a season, in a single run. When a run is gone, it is gone until next year.

  2. ii.

    Colour from plants

    Madder, weld, walnut and indigo. No synthetic dyes, so no two batches come out quite the same.

  3. iii.

    Made to be mended

    Generous seams to let out or take in, and spare thread and buttons with every piece.

II — The season

Two landscapes, one season.

Chapter I

Madder Valley

Dye fields below the ridge, at the last hour of light.

The valley is where the colour comes from. Madder grows in the lower fields, walnut trees line the river, and weld seeds itself along every wall. This chapter is cut from wool and heavy linen, and dyed in all three.

Boiled wool, madder-dyed linen, wool crêpe, lambswool · 6 pieces

Chapter II

Salt Coast

Where the river meets the sea, and the light goes silver.

Downriver the colours thin out. Flax grows on the coast plain, the wind takes the edge off everything, and indigo is the only dye strong enough to hold its own against the salt.

III — The making

Colour that grew somewhere.

Every colour this season came out of a plant. Plant dyes never land in exactly the same place twice, so neither do the clothes. We would rather tell you that than hide it.

  • MadderRoots, dug in their third year, for the reds.
  • WeldThe whole plant, cut in flower, for yellow.
  • WalnutGreen hulls, gathered in autumn, for brown.
  • IndigoLeaves, fermented in a vat, for the one deep blue.
